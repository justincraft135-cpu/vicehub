Which edition should you buy?

For most players, the honest answer is the Standard edition. It contains the full game, and GTA launches are about the game itself: the story, the world and the first weeks of exploring it. At a reported $79.99 it is already a premium-priced release, and everything essential is in the box.

The Ultimate edition, reported at $99.99, adds extra vehicles, weapons and story content on top of the base game. That $20 difference is worth it if you know you will spend hundreds of hours in Leonida and want the extra content from day one, or if you simply want the most complete version of a game you have waited over a decade for. It is harder to justify if you are unsure how much you will play, because the core experience is identical. Neither edition's bonus content has been fully detailed by Rockstar, and the edition extras listed in the table above come from retail reporting Reported, so check the store listing again at checkout and make sure what is included still matches what you are paying for.

A note on the preorder bonus

Both editions are reported to include the Vintage Vice City Pack as a preorder bonus. Preorder bonuses in GTA games are typically cosmetic or early-unlock items rather than essential content, and nothing reported so far suggests this pack changes the story. If you are reading this after preorders have been open a while, do not pay over the odds to a reseller for a "bonus edition" — the bonus is tied to preordering from a normal store, not to scarce physical stock.

How preordering actually works

A few practical points that catch people out every big launch:

  • When you pay depends on the store. Digital stores typically charge immediately or shortly before release, while physical retailers often charge when your copy ships. Check the store's own preorder terms before ordering, especially if you are ordering months of expenses around it.
  • You can usually cancel. Most major stores let you cancel a preorder any time before it charges or ships. That makes preordering low-risk if your plans or the reviews change, but the exact rules are each store's, so read them at checkout.
  • Preloading is a digital benefit. Only digital copies can preload from November 12. A physical copy cannot be installed until it is in your hands, and even then, expect a download on top.
  • Watch for code-in-a-box. Some physical editions, particularly on Xbox, may contain a download code rather than a disc. The table above flags formats per store, and the listing itself will say. If a real disc matters to you, for resale or lending, verify before you buy.
  • Beware of listings that look too cheap. For a launch this big, scam listings and key resellers offering impossible prices appear every time. Stick to the stores in the table above or your console's own store, where the price should match the reported $79.99 and $99.99.

Digital vs physical, in more depth

The short version above is right, but here is the fuller trade-off. Go digital if your priority is playing at the earliest possible moment: you preload on November 12, the game unlocks without a disc, and there is no delivery to wait for. Digital is also the only sensible option for Xbox Series S owners and anyone with a PS5 Digital Edition, since those consoles have no disc drive at all. The downsides are that you cannot resell or lend the game, and you are trusting your connection with a very large download — our file size guide has download-time estimates for different connection speeds.

Go physical if you like owning a disc, collecting boxed games, or having the option to trade the game in later. The risks are delivery timing and stock: for a launch this anticipated, some retailers' deliveries slip past launch day, and midnight store openings are not guaranteed everywhere. If you go physical, order from a store that explicitly promises release-day delivery, and treat the disc as the start of the install rather than the whole game, because a day-one download is still likely. Whichever route you choose, run through the launch-day checklist in the final week so the boring parts — storage, updates, subscriptions — are already done.
